### Detailed Justification for Potential Global GDP Losses Due to Quantum Security Issues

#### Introduction
The advent of quantum computing heralds a new era of computational power, capable of solving complex problems far beyond the reach of classical computers. However, this progress also introduces significant vulnerabilities in current cryptographic systems, which underpin global financial systems, communications, and critical infrastructure. The potential for quantum computers to break widely-used cryptographic protocols poses a grave threat, with estimated global economic losses spanning trillions of dollars due to quantum security breaches. This analysis delves into the multifaceted risks posed by quantum computing on global security and economies, and suggests crucial internal audit procedures to mitigate these threats.

#### The Cryptographic Crisis Faceing Global Systems
1. **Current Cryptographic Standards Vulnerability**:
   Modern cryptographic systems, such as RSA, ECC (Elliptic Curve Cryptography), and various hash functions, rely on the computational difficulty of certain mathematical problems (e.g., factoring large integers, solving discrete logarithms). Quantum computers, utilizing Shor's algorithm, can solve these problems exponentially faster, rendering existing cryptographic protocols obsolete and vulnerable to quantum attacks.

2. **Impact on Financial Systems**:
   - **Banking and Finance**: The majority of financial transactions globally rely on secure cryptographic communications. A breach could lead to unauthorized transactions, theft of sensitive financial data, and loss of investor confidence, impacting stock markets and leading to significant economic instability.
   - **Insurance and Reinsurance**: Quantum attacks could compromise the integrity of policy records, claims processing, and underwriting, disrupting entire sectors reliant on precise and secure data storage and transaction processing.

3. **Infrastructure and Critical Services**:
   - **Energy Grid**: Smart grids rely on secure communication to optimize energy distribution and manage microgrids. Quantum attacks could disrupt power supply, leading to widespread blackouts and economic loss.
   - **Supply Chain and Logistics**: Quantum computing could compromise the cryptographic safeguards in supply chain management systems, leading to disruptions, loss of inventory data, and severe economic ramifications.

4. **Government and Defense Implications**:
   - **National Security**: Quantum capabilities could undermine national defense systems’ cryptographic foundations, potentially leading to espionage, sabotage, and loss of strategic information.
   - **Cybersecurity**: Government agencies and critical infrastructure operators face heightened risks of cyber-attacks, compromising public safety and national security, potentially leading to costly recovery efforts and long-term damage to public trust.

#### Economic Impact Analysis
- **Direct Economic Losses**: immediate losses from compromised transactions, theft, and fraud, estimated in the tens of billions to trillions of dollars, depending on the scope and scale of breaches.
- **Indirect Economic Consequences**: damage to market confidence, reduced investment, decreased consumer spending, lower economic growth rates, and increased insurance premiums.
- **Long-term Economic Effects**: erosion of innovation capabilities due to reduced confidence in data security, potential divestment from affected industries, and long-term shifts in global economic power as nations with robust quantum defenses gain advantages.

#### Estimated Global GDP Losses
- According to reports from quantifiable risk assessments and simulations, global GDP could face losses ranging from **$2 trillion to $3.3 trillion** annually due to quantum-related security breaches by 2030, escalating to even higher figures as quantum technologies mature and find more widespread application.

#### Recommended Internal Audit Procedures
To mitigate these risks, organizations and governments must undertake comprehensive internal audit procedures focusing on:

1. **Cryptographic Audits**:
   - Regular assessment of cryptographic implementations to ensure use of quantum-resistant algorithms where feasible.
   - Testing current cryptographic systems against potential quantum attacks using simulated quantum environments.

2. **Risk Assessment Frameworks**:
   - Implementing frameworks to identify and quantify quantum-related risks across all operational domains.
   - Continuous monitoring of quantum computing advancements and adjusting risk assessments accordingly.

3. **Transition Strategies**:
   - Development and implementation of transition plans to migrate to post-quantum cryptographic standards.
   - Allocation of dedicated resources and timelines for upgrading cryptographic infrastructures.

4. **Security Training and Awareness**:
   - Enhanced training programs for IT staff and decision-makers on quantum threats and mitigation strategies.
   - Increased awareness campaigns targeting stakeholders across all levels of the organization.

5. **Vendor and Supply Chain Security**:
   - Rigorous vetting of third-party vendors for cryptographic security, ensuring alignment with quantum-resistant standards.
   - Supply chain audits focusing on encryption practices and quantum security readiness of all tiers.

6. **Regulatory Compliance**:
   - Staying abreast of and complying with emerging international standards and regulations regarding quantum security.
   - Engaging with policymakers to contribute to the shaping of quantum-resilient cybersecurity frameworks.

### Conclusion
The potential for quantum computing to breach current cryptographic systems poses an existential threat to global financial stability and economic integrity. Immediate action through comprehensive audits, robust risk management strategies, and preparedness planning is paramount. By proactively addressing these vulnerabilities, organizations and governments can safeguard against the looming quantum security challenges and protect economic stability in the quantum era.
